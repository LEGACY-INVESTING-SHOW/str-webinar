## 3. The four jobs

Heading: Money in a brokerage account has one job. The right short-term rental has four.

Once you earn $150,000 or more, the question is no longer whether you invest. You already fund the 401(k) and send the rest to a brokerage account. The question is what each dollar does after it leaves your checking account.

**Left column: $50,000 into the market**

You own $50,000 worth of shares. Their only job is to be worth more later. Nothing pays you in the meantime, and nothing about the position changes your tax bill this year.

**Right column: $50,000 toward one short-term rental**

The $50,000 becomes the down payment on a much larger asset, and that asset can work four ways at once.

1. Cash flow. Guests pay you every month after the mortgage, taxes, insurance, cleaning, and platform fees.
2. Equity. The guests' payments cover the mortgage, so the loan balance drops while you hold the property.
3. Appreciation. If the property rises in value, you own that upside.
4. Depreciation. Real estate creates a paper loss even while it makes money. If you operate the rental yourself, materially participate, and meet the requirements, that loss can reduce the tax on your W-2 income.

Preston built his portfolio this way, and he does not tell you to buy seven Airbnbs. He shows you how to find one property where all four jobs pencil out.

## 4. The IRS gets paid first

Heading: Most of your investing starts after the IRS has already been paid.

For a W-2 earner the order is fixed: you earn, tax comes out of the paycheck, and whatever is left goes to the 401(k), the brokerage account, and the index funds.

Those investments are fine. They only ever touch the money that survived the tax bill.

A short-term rental you materially participate in can sit inside the tax strategy instead of after it. The depreciation can reduce the tax on income you already earned at your job this year. If you pay $40,000 or $50,000 a year in federal income tax, that is a number worth checking before you decide where the next $50,000 goes.

## 9. Three questions people ask before they register

**I have a full-time job. Can I materially participate?**
The tests count your hours and involvement in the property, not your job title. Preston walks through the tests and what to log so the deduction holds up.

**Do I need seven properties?**
No. The example above is one property. Preston's point is one property where the numbers work, bought with the money you were going to invest anyway.

**Is the tax part legal?**
The short-term rental exception, material participation, cost segregation, and bonus depreciation are all in the tax code. The result depends on meeting the tests and keeping records, which is most of what Preston covers. Nothing on this page is tax advice for your situation.
